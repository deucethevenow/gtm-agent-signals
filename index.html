2 Analyze the Situation

"What's really going on here? Why did they go quiet?"

🧠 Situation Analysis Questions

Why did they go quiet?
  • □ Waiting on internal approval?
  • □ Budget timing issue?
  • □ Evaluating competitors?
  • □ Wrong stakeholder?
  • □ Priority shifted?
  • □ Just busy / forgot?
What's blocking next stage?
  • □ Need more information?
  • □ Need executive buy-in?
  • □ Need ROI proof?
  • □ Need pricing flexibility?
  • □ Need timeline adjustment?
What leverage do we have?
  • □ Past success to reference?
  • □ New inventory they'd want?
  • □ Company news to hook on?
  • □ Deadline/urgency to create?
  • □ Relationship to lean on?
What's the right approach?
  • □ Provide value / ammo for internal sell?
  • □ Create urgency?
  • □ Offer flexibility?
  • □ Direct ask for decision?
  • □ Light touch / easy out?

3 Craft the Perfect Next Email

"What's the one email that moves this deal forward?"

📤 Output to Slack for Rep Approval

Subject:
Quick ROI summary for your March 15 review
Hi Sarah,

I know budget approvals are coming up, so I put together a quick one-pager summarizing the results from your past two campaigns with us:

• 87% organizer satisfaction
• 12,000 samples distributed
• $2.31 cost per trial (vs. $4-6 industry avg)

Given the new protein bar launch, timing on Q2 fitness events is tight — happy to hold the 15 Bay Area dates through Friday if that helps your internal timeline.

Let me know if you need anything else for the March 15 review.
💡 Why This Approach

Context: Sarah mentioned needing to prove ROI for budget approval by March 15. Proposal sent 12 days ago, no response.

Strategy: Rather than generic follow-up, provide ammunition (past results) she can use internally. Light deadline creates action without pressure.

Signals Used:

Past campaign results (87% NPS)
Meeting note: "March 15 budget deadline"
Company news: New product launch
